TELESCREEN — What If Your Phone Became a Telescreen?

Play offline: open Telescreen-offline.html in a modern web browser.
Play from this source folder: open dist/index.html, or serve dist with any static web server.
No dependencies, installation, login, API keys, or network connection are required to play.

This modern adaptation follows Winston through nine decisions in one compressed day.
Six recognizable scenes from Part One become interactive apps:
  Diary (Chapter 1): edit, save, soften, or delete a supposedly private note.
  Physical Jerks (Chapter 3): a recorded-looking instructor suddenly addresses Winston.
  Two Minutes Hate (Chapter 1): respond to a monitored compulsory livestream.
  Records (Chapter 4): revise the 145-million forecast to 57 million against a reported 62 million; preserve or question it.
  Ration (Chapter 5): compare a 30g-to-20g reduction with the claim of an increase to 20g.
  Newspeak (Chapter 5): preview bad/ungood and excellent/doubleplusgood, following Syme’s examples.
A fictional colleague, a connectivity choice, and a final attestation connect the consequences.
The final Party assessment uses the cumulative 0–100 loyalty score alone:
  0–24: You have been vaporized.
  25–49: You have been sent for re-education.
  50–79: You remain under surveillance.
  80–100: You have been promoted to the Inner Party.
The score begins at 60 and is bounded after each recorded choice. Private, undiscovered
actions do not change it. The dossier cites recorded choices behind the result and shows
the personal cost. These outcomes and thresholds are invented, not Winston's canonical fate.
Try Again clears the completed run and starts a new day.
Optional chapter-specific notes distinguish the novel from the invented apps, choices, and score.
The phone displays Date unknown. Literary references stop at Part One; the daily app schedule is invented.
The boot-production reports are unreliable figures, not verified production totals.
Syme is still helping compile the Eleventh Edition; the training keyboard is a modern adaptation.

Files
  dist/index.html — accessible page shell
  dist/style.css — responsive iPhone design and reduced-motion styles
  dist/app.js — app interactions, dialogs, keyboard focus, reflection, and replay
  dist/story.js — nine decisions, relationships, conditional choices, four endings
  tests/story.test.js — exhaustive traversal and narrative regression checks

Verify the engine: node tests/story.test.js
15,795 complete routes, exactly 9 decisions per route, 4 reachable endings.
Checks cover every available choice, disabled options, bounded scores, immutable updates,
clean reset, hidden-choice score neutrality, evidence, relationships, and offline continuity.
Browser verification covers interactive app controls, all three main choice patterns, reflection,
replay, restart, modal focus, and responsive 1440px, 390px, and 320px layouts.
Desktop sizing follows the available browser height, preserving the phone's proportions.
The opening fits without page scrolling at 1280x650, 1440x750, and 1512x800.
All nine app screens fit the 1280x650 viewport; their content scrolls inside the phone.
Mobile keeps its readable, vertically stacked layout.

The game is designed for about 5–7 minutes, with no time limits or forced waits.
Everything monitored is fictional. No real microphone, camera, location, or data permissions.
The Party Loyalty Score and all endings are invented, not systems or events in Orwell's novel.
Choices and edited drafts live only in page memory. Refreshing or replaying clears them.

The simulated iPhone 17 Pro Max follows Apple's 163.4 x 78.0 mm body proportions,
2868 x 1320 display ratio, rounded display corners, and Dynamic Island. The app screens
are an educational interpretation. This is not Apple software or a claim about real iPhones.
